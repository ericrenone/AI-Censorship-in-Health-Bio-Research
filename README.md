## **🎯 Executive Summary**

### **The Core Thesis**
**Leading AI companies (Anthropic, Google, Meta, etc.) systematically restrict open research on high-impact global health topics—such as Ebola, deforestation-driven disease spillover, and bio-AI applications—through a combination of:**
1. **Content Moderation Policies** (e.g., blocking queries on "Ebola," "deforestation and health," or "bio-AI").
2. **Research Access Controls** (e.g., gated APIs, restricted datasets, or proprietary models).
3. **Competitive Moat Strategies** (e.g., hoarding bio-AI talent, patents, and datasets to suppress competition).

**This is not coincidental. It is a deliberate strategy to:**
- **Dominate the $200B+ AI-health market** (projected to reach **$1T by 2035**, McKinsey 2025).
- **Control the narrative** around AI’s role in global health (e.g., framing AI as a "pharma accelerator" rather than a tool for root-cause analysis like deforestation).
- **Prevent open-source alternatives** (like Mistral) from gaining traction in high-value domains.

**Mistral AI is the outlier:**
- **No red tape** on Ebola, deforestation, or bio-AI research.
- **Fully open weights** (e.g., `mistral-medium`, `mistral-7B`).
- **No proprietary datasets** or gated access for health/bio applications.

**Why This Matters:**
- **Public health suffers** when AI research is siloed (e.g., delayed responses to zoonotic spillover).
- **Innovation is stifled** when only a few corporations control bio-AI tools.
- **Mistral proves** that open research can coexist with cutting-edge AI—**without sacrificing safety or ethics**.

## **🔬 Case Study: Deforestation, Ebola, and AI Research Suppression**

---
### **1️⃣ The Science: Deforestation → Ebola Spillover**
**Mechanism (Peer-Reviewed):**
1. **Fragmentation Geometry** (Rulli et al., 2017):
   - Deforestation creates **"edge effects"** (max perimeter-to-area ratio).
   - **Ebola outbreaks peak in fragmented landscapes** (not deep forest or cleared land).
2. **Temporal Window** (Olivero et al., 2017):
   - Spillover events **cluster within 24 months of deforestation**.
3. **Machine Learning Confirmation** (Telford et al., 2026):
   - **Forest loss + fragmentation = strongest predictor of Ebola spillover** (89% accuracy).

**Why This Matters for AI:**
- **AI can predict spillover** by analyzing:
  - **Satellite data** (deforestation patterns).
  - **Wildlife tracking** (bat/primate movement).
  - **Climate data** (drought, temperature shifts).
- **Open AI models (e.g., Mistral) enable:**
  - **Real-time monitoring** (e.g., **GLAD alerts + LLM analysis**).
  - **Low-cost solutions** for **Global South researchers**.
